I am a Master's student in computational medicinal chemistry, working at the intersection of medicinal chemistry and structure-based drug design. My research centres on Candida albicans Als3 (PDB: 4LEB), a cell-surface adhesin involved in biofilm formation, as a target for new antifungal and antibiofilm agents. Across two manuscripts I have combined large-scale molecular docking, DFT/HOMO–LUMO analysis, scaffold classification, and a dual-engine consensus docking framework (PharmaCoNet + AutoDock Vina) to screen, validate, and rationalise candidate compounds against this target.

My undergraduate training was at MSA University in 6th of October City, Egypt, on a programme validated by the University of Greenwich. The work was examined to Greenwich standards and moderated by Greenwich academics, so I hold a British BSc (Hons) in addition to the Egyptian degree, awarded with First Class Honours. Every exam was sat in Egypt — the degree is genuinely British, the four years were genuinely Egyptian, and I think that combination says something useful about how I learned to work: to an external standard, without the external address.

I am currently pursuing my M.Sc. in the Department of Chemical Engineering at Yeungnam University, South Korea, funded by the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS). I also serve as a peer reviewer, most recently for an A. baumannii docking manuscript, and I am preparing to apply for a DPhil in bioinformatics or synthetic biology at the University of Oxford.
